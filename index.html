Field Guide

Where the footprint hides.

OPTIONAL READING
≈ 4 MIN

Materials- vs. Use-Intensive

Ulrich & Eppinger separate materials-intensive products (impact concentrated in making and disposing) from use-intensive products (impact concentrated in operation). A drone can be either one. A hobby drone flown 35 hours a year is materials-intensive. A utility inspection drone flown 600 hours a year is use-intensive, and its batteries wear out over and over.

The Physics You'll Feel

Mass → power. Hover power rises with mass. Every gram of frame, motor, or battery costs energy on every flight.

Battery size has diminishing returns. A bigger pack adds flight time, but it also adds mass, which raises the power needed. Watch the flight-time readout as you slide the capacity.

Cycle life is a footprint. Making a battery pack emits roughly 60–90 kg CO₂e per kWh. A pack rated for 300 cycles on a drone that flies 3,000 hours gets replaced many times.

Two Life Cycles

McDonough & Braungart: technical materials should circulate in industry; biological materials should return safely to nature. Anything that fits neither (glued multi-material assemblies, unlabeled plastics) is waste by design. And nothing circulates unless the product actually comes back.

Beware the heroic material.

A bio-based frame paired with a battery that wears out every 300 cycles and no take-back program isn't circular. It's a press release. Coherence across the whole system is what scores.
